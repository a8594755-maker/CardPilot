# Retained allocation decision

The current four endpoints were compared with their actual connected2M parents,
not the older detached controls. Existing raw integer-chip moments match both
independent terminal reviews. Runtime inventories match; cohort IDs are disjoint.

Session-level intervals also include zero. Shared parent cohorts make these
contrasts dependent. They are descriptive, unadjusted cross-cohort comparisons,
not paired-deal causal effects or a population claim from two training seeds.
Seed1 recent versus control looked better largely because the control point
estimate fell; recent itself is nearly unchanged from its parent. This removes
the rationale for selecting it on that relative score alone.

Measured training subprocess throughput was approximately 615 physical hands/s
for the prior unchanged continuation and 319 hands/s for the recent-pool trial,
which included observed resource contention. These are not controlled algorithm
speed comparisons. At those aggregate rates, 2.7B additional physical executions
would take roughly 51 to 98 continuous days BEFORE evaluations or outages, for
one aggregate budget rather than 2.7B per seed/control. This is an extrapolation,
not a benchmark or a guarantee that effective sample coverage scales similarly.

Allocation: do not purchase more external precision on these same endpoints or
automatically scale four branches toward paper scale. Preserve both recent and
control endpoints. The current evidence neither establishes positive slopes nor
falsifies eventual larger-scale improvement. Before the next expensive run,
inspect the actual learning objective and training distribution against the
execution contract, using prior gradient/greedy audits to avoid repeating them;
choose one substantive training hypothesis and a matched control, not another
endpoint ranking exercise. A larger fixed-dose control remains admissible if
that assessment finds no mechanistic defect and its information justifies cost.
